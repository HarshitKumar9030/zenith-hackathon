# WasteWise: assisted collection, verified recovery

Revised business model · 23 October 2026 · India · proposal for validation, not a profitability forecast

## 1. The recommendation

Build a neighbourhood collection service with AI-assisted sorting, rather than a recycling factory or continuous household surveillance system. Start inside one apartment complex or campus, partner with existing collectors and processors, and earn primarily from dependable collection subscriptions. Share actual recovered-material proceeds transparently.

My assessment: the original idea is an 8/10 problem-and-product concept, but approximately 5/10 as an operating model because collection costs, hardware costs, processing contracts, and payout accounting are unresolved. The revision improves those weaknesses; its viability still depends on a paid pilot.

**Promise:** “Put it aside. We help identify it, collect it on your building’s route, and show you what happened to it.”

The pain point is the repeated effort of identifying, sorting, finding a collector, negotiating prices, and trusting the weighing and payment. AI reduces those steps; reliable collection and transparent settlement are what customers buy.

## 2. Who to serve first

Start with one dense building or campus containing 50–100 interested households, subject to paid signups. Use existing waste workers as trained collection partners, with protective equipment, clear responsibilities, guaranteed collection pay, and transparent bonuses.

Serve households and the building manager. The manager is a second customer for scheduled collection, segregation reporting, and common-area cleanliness. Do not assume a household subscription replaces municipal collection or that a building will pay before a contract exists.

Avoid a citywide launch, scattered doorstep visits, factory waste, and owning processing equipment during the pilot.

## 3. Three separate material streams

| Stream | Service | Commercial treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Clean dry recyclables | Scheduled route pickup; separate paper, plastic, glass and metal | Quote by material and grade; settle after weighing and buyer acceptance |
| Organics | Separate common-area drop-off with an agreed frequent collection arrangement | Building-funded processing contract; no automatic household cash promise |
| Batteries and electronics | Separately booked specialist collection | Partner quote and handling requirements; never mixed with organics |

Daily organic waste needs frequent handling. Do not ask residents to store wet food waste until an infrequent dry-recyclables pickup. If the building already has suitable organic collection, integrate with it rather than charging for a duplicate service.

## 4. Revised Gold and Platinum subscriptions

Keep the original prices as pilot hypotheses, but reduce the expensive doorstep promise.

| Feature | Gold | Platinum |
|---|---:|---:|
| Proposed monthly price | ₹559 | ₹859 |
| Included dry-recyclable pickups | 8 | 12 |
| Maximum per included pickup | 4 kg | 6 kg |
| Total monthly included weight | 24 kg | 48 kg |
| Scheduling | Choose available building-route windows | More route-window choices and priority rescheduling |
| Identification and editable AI checklist | Included | Included |

| Bulky items and specialist waste | Separate quote | Separate quote |

Both the per-pickup and monthly cap apply. Include ordinary metal in those caps: excluding metal creates an unbounded transport obligation. Heavy appliances require a quote even below a nominal weight threshold because handling is different.

Count credits only after successful handover. Failed company pickups restore the credit. Show cancellation and resident no-show rules before purchase. Do not promise arbitrary appointment times, unlimited rescheduling, or daily service at these prices.

Your original 14 and 24 pickups leave only ₹39.93 and ₹35.79 of subscription revenue per pickup, respectively, before any expense. If a completed stop costs ₹40, the subscriptions already fail before support, technology, or taxes. The revised plans leave ₹69.88 and ₹71.58 per included pickup.

If 14/24 pickups are essential, price them only after measured route costs justify the promise. More frequent building drop-offs can coexist with fewer doorstep collections.

## 5. Low-effort identification without mandatory cameras

### Default workflow

1. Resident uploads a photo, writes a description, or uses the building’s shared capture station.
2. Gemini suggests an editable checklist: item, possible material, contamination, mixed components and questions. OCR reads visible packaging text.
3. The resident confirms or corrects the checklist. Uncertain entries stay uncertain.
4. The supplied locality guide determines supported segregation and preparation instructions, with the exact source passage.
5. The resident chooses an available collection window. Submission and confirmation times are recorded separately.
6. The collector verifies material and condition, tares the container, records measured weight and provides an itemized receipt.
7. The resident accepts the receipt or opens a dispute.
8. The downstream partner accepts the batch; the settlement ledger records actual proceeds and any documented deductions.

AI estimates are not measurements, accepted grades, verified disposal, or money owed. A fixed camera cannot reliably see through opaque bags or establish a precise weight or battery chemistry.

### Optional shared camera

Pilot one clearly marked camera aimed only at a controlled sorting surface. Use event-triggered or scheduled snapshots instead of continuous video inference. Associate a submission using a QR code or deliberate check-in, not facial recognition. Require resident confirmation before booking or assigning value.

Limit the field of view, prevent capture of homes or unrelated public areas, obtain appropriate consent, restrict staff access, and delete captures according to a documented short retention policy. Test whether the station actually reduces effort compared with a phone photo before buying more hardware.

The building pays an agreed station fee covering hardware, installation, maintenance, connectivity and inference. Do not silently subsidize cameras through the ₹559 plan.

## 6. A workable payout model

Separate collection income from recovered-material income. Do not split subscriptions using scrap percentages.

### Subscription revenue

Pays for route labour, transport, consumables, support, software and company overhead. Collectors receive guaranteed pay for completed work, not only a percentage of whatever scrap happens to be valuable.

### Recovered-material settlement

Proposed pilot distribution of a **positive net recovery pool**:

- Resident: **70%**.
- Collection-partner bonus pool: **10%**, additional to guaranteed route pay.
- WasteWise: **20%**, before company overhead and tax.

Define the pool as actual buyer proceeds minus documented, batch-attributable processing charges, downstream delivery charges, and agreed disposal deductions. Publish the deduction schedule in advance. Never charge a cost twice or hide general company expenses in a resident’s deduction.

Example: a batch realizes ₹1,000; contracted processing and downstream delivery total ₹200. The ₹800 pool yields ₹560 to residents, ₹80 in collector bonuses and ₹160 to WasteWise. These are illustrative assumptions, not a buyer quote. Allocate the batch fairly using verified material-specific weights and contracted grade prices, with an auditable breakdown.

A processor buying scrap is a buyer. A processor charging for treatment is a vendor. Do not automatically give either one 15% of every sale. Your original 35/40/15/10 split sums correctly, but the last 10% is a revenue allocation, not guaranteed profit.

Show an estimate before handover and settle only accepted proceeds. Never silently turn a negative-value item into a resident charge: obtain approval first. The user must understand that the subscription buys convenience and traceability; compare their net scrap return against local direct-sale alternatives during the pilot.

Do not apply lead-acid prices to lithium-ion cells or ordinary household batteries. There is no defensible single “factory waste price”: specify the material, chemistry, condition, contamination, contract and handling costs. Unknown or hazardous streams should display “partner quote required.”

Your ₹25–55/kg plastic figure is a proposed **processing cost**, not a sale price. Obtain processor quotes and establish who owns and sells the output. Do not subtract processing costs from a scrap sale that already transfers processing responsibility to the buyer.

Your organic assumption, 5 kg input to 1.6 kg compost, implies a 32% mass yield; it needs a measured local trial. Selling that output for ₹150 requires ₹93.75/kg, not a safe bulk-commodity assumption. At the cited packaged example of ₹14/kg, 1.6 kg brings only ₹22.40 gross. If processing alone costs ₹30, that batch loses ₹7.60 before collection and other expenses. This comparison is illustrative; the retail listing is not a wholesale buyer quote.

Keep compost as a contracted service or a separately validated business. Do not promise household organic earnings before yield, quality, storage, processing time, and offtake are established.

Battery collection requires a suitable specialist chain. CPCB publishes registration and handling guidance through its [battery EPR portal](https://eprbattery.cpcb.gov.in/). Verify applicable requirements and partner registrations before operating; do not present ordinary collectors as hazardous-waste processors.

These figures exclude acquisition costs, camera hardware, organic service, exceptional handling, refunds and taxes. Tax-inclusive prices need recalculation after applicable treatment is determined. Contribution is not net profit. Do not count scrap resale as both subscription income and company income: only the retained share belongs in WasteWise’s recovery margin, subject to the appropriate accounting treatment.

Measure both actual usage and full-entitlement economics. An apparent profit caused solely by residents forgetting to use credits is not a strong service model.

## 9. Dashboards and permissions

### Resident

Upcoming window; remaining pickup credits; editable AI checklist; clarification prompts; source-backed preparation; collector identity; actual weight and grade; itemized receipts; estimated versus pending versus settled recovery earnings; payout history; dispute status; privacy settings.

### Collection partner

Assigned route and windows; only necessary addresses; confirmed checklists; actual material/condition corrections; tare and net weight entry; receipt acceptance; exceptions and safety flags; guaranteed work pay; pending and settled bonuses; payout status. A collector must never approve their own disputed weight or settlement.

### Operations console

Building-level routing and capacity; plan entitlements; partner status; versioned prices/contracts; processor acceptance; disputes; settlement audit trail; subscription receipts; collector pay; direct service cost; retained recovery margin; contribution; fixed expenses; separately labelled profit/loss.

Two customer-facing dashboards still require this internal operations capability. Avoid publishing a “revenue” total that mixes residents’ payable balances with platform earnings.

## 10. Local leaderboard and trust

Make leaderboard participation opt-in and use building/locality identifiers rather than public precise GPS. Show aliases. Reward verified segregation consistency and reliable participation rather than simply producing the most waste. Include transparent scoring and eligibility rules; normalize comparisons where appropriate.

Use acceptance receipts for verified recycling quantities. AI detection alone earns no “recycled” claim. Avoid CO₂ savings unless a defensible methodology, factors and boundaries are supplied. Keep streaks optional and never financially penalize people for missing one.

Use accessible checkboxes, clear confirmation states, animated tabs and restrained status transitions. Motion must not imply that weighing, payment or processing has happened when it has not.

## 11. Architecture boundaries

- Capture layer: phone photo, text or optional station events.
- Identification: Gemini image understanding, OCR, and a later evaluated custom-model adapter.
- Confirmation: editable checklist and unresolved questions.
- Guide engine: locality/version, source passages, conditional rules and uncertainty.
- Operations: subscriptions, pickup credits, capacity, assignment, measured weight and disputes.
- Commercial layer: versioned quotes, acceptance, batch allocation, settlement and payouts.
- Audit layer: actor, timestamp, prior value, new value and reason for every consequential change.

For local development, MongoDB can store these records. Keep provider credentials server-side and camera ingestion authenticated. Subscription renewal and payout events need idempotent processing; client checkboxes must not determine subscription entitlement, collector role, final weight or payable balance. Hosting, payment-provider and production-security choices follow the pilot requirements.

## 12. What to demonstrate in 24 hours

Use one locality, one actual supplied guide and 15–20 common items. Demonstrate photo/text or one shared-camera capture; AI-generated checkboxes; mixed-material and blurry-photo clarification; resident confirmation; scheduled pickup; collector correction and measured weight; source-backed preparation; a receipt; and an illustrative settlement with explicit demo labels.

Show role-specific dashboards and a versioned rate card. Payment/subscription simulations must say “demo”; do not simulate a real payout as settled cash. Defer fleet optimization, continuous video, automatic payouts, factory processing, multiple cities and hardware deployment.

## 13. Four-week paid pilot and decision gates

Before launch, secure a building agreement, route capacity, a suitable downstream buyer, an organic-service arrangement if offered, and a separate specialist partner for batteries/e-waste. Validate prices, reject handling, worker protections and camera consent.

Measure: paid conversion and renewal intent; completed-stop cost; time and distance per route; missed pickups; contamination/rejection; weighing disputes; time to settlement; all-in collector compensation; gross receipts versus retained margin; privacy complaints.

Proposed internal gates, to be adjusted with partners: at least 95% of pickups completed in their promised window; fewer than 5% disputed receipts; positive contribution after direct costs at full included usage; guaranteed partner compensation paid on time; and evidence that households will actually pay the proposed price. These are targets, not achieved metrics or industry benchmarks.

Expand only after the pilot demonstrates service reliability, fair worker income, genuine willingness to pay and sustainable costs.

## Final positioning

**WasteWise is a trusted neighbourhood collection and recovery service: AI removes sorting friction, people verify reality, and transparent records connect the household to the collector and processor.**

Its defensible advantage should be reliable local operations and trusted settlement. The camera, model, animations and leaderboard support that service; they are not the business model by themselves.
